# 04 — Quarterly Estimates

*The part engineers call "pre-loading." You're paying tax in quarterly
installments during the year, because no employer is withholding for you. Also:
the absolute calmest chapter, promise.*

## Why "quarterly" exists

When you have a W-2 job, taxes leave each paycheck automatically. When money
arrives as royalties, freelance checks, or reseller profits, nothing is being
withheld — so the tax system asks you to **pre-pay, as roughly-you-go**, in
four installments during the year. Skip it, and there may be a (modest, but
annoying) underpayment penalty added on top of what you owe in April —
*separate from* just owing a big lump sum.

"Quarterly" is misleading — the due dates aren't evenly spaced calendar
quarters. They're published on IRS.gov each year; mark them in your calendar
app with advance reminders. Search "**estimated tax for individuals**" on
IRS.gov for current dates and the current rules.

## The two mental models

### Model A: "the operating manager" (what to *set aside*)

Every time business money lands, immediately move a slice into a dedicated
tax account *before* you can feel rich. Then, on the IRS's quarterly dates,
send that account's contents (or a hand-computed estimate) to the IRS.

Beginners usually pick a set-aside percentage that "feels honest" for their
bracket mix, then fine-tune with a real pro based on last year's return and
next year's expected profit. The right % is a person-specific, pro-validated
number — which is exactly why this guide doesn't print one.

### Model B: "the accountant" (what to *compute*)

IRS.gov publishes two worksheets (One in the 1040-ES booklet, one in Form
2210 for shortfalls). They're learnable but tedious. This book's honest
recommendation: **use tax-estimate software or a tax pro** to compute
installment amounts, especially in year one. You *can* DIY; you don't *have*
to, and the price of a pro consult here often pays for itself by avoiding
penalties. (The Formula-is-simple version is: "roughly match last year's total
tax when your income is similar" — but that safe-harbor rule has conditions;
verify the current version.)

## The calm priority ladder

When money lands:

1. *First*, the tax slice goes to the tax account. Not "after ads" or "after
   rent." First, because later is a myth.
2. *Second*, expenses get paid from working capital.
3. *Third*, you draw a living. Owner's glory is last. This is boring and
   correct.

## The yearly rhythm (ADHD-friendly)

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| Every royalty/sale deposit | Move the slice. 60 seconds. |
| Last day of each month | Chapter 02's 10-min ritual. Update totals. |
| Quarterly due dates | Pay estimates from the tax account. Log it. |
| January | Reconcile 1099s (Ch. 02), collect tax slips, book pro appt. |
| Filing season | File 1040 + Schedule C + SE. Reconcile final numbers. Adjust slice % if needed for next year. |

Setting four recurring calendar alarms named "ESTIMATE — do the thing" today
is a form of adulting that beats any productivity book.

## Where to actually pay

IRS.gov lets you pay online from a bank account (free-ish, secure) or card
(with fees). Some states also want state estimated payments — *ask your pro*,
because state thresholds and rules vary wildly and this guide isn't your
state's legal manual.

Record-keeping for estimates: save the confirmation emails/PDFs. In April,
you report "estimated payments already made" on your 1040, and weird errors
(transposed digits, misdated credits) are much easier to fix when evidence
lives in a folder called "estimates."

## The three scary situations, defused

- **"Q1 was huge, Q3 was nothing."** Estimates are paid on the *dates*, but
  some safe-harbor and penalty computations evaluate income unevenly
  (annualized income methods exist). Not DIYable in five minutes — a
  legitimate pro reason, listed in Chapter 06.
- **"I forgot to pay a quarter entirely."** Pay late or as soon as you
  remember; penalties are computed per-period, not all-or-nothing. Don't
  doomspiral; handle it and move on.
- **"My profit is negative this year / nothing earned."** You generally owe
  little or no estimated tax in such years — but the exact logic depends on
  your overall tax picture (other jobs, spouse's income, credits). Confirm
  with a pro before *silently skipping* because vibes said "nothing."

## A page you'll want to copy into your calendar

```text
On each IRS quarterly date:
  [ ] Check tax account balance
  [ ] Pay estimate online (IRS Direct Pay / EFTPS)
  [ ] Save confirmation PDF into "estimates" folder
  [ ] Post payment in my year-to-date tracker
```
