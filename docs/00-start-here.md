# 00 — Start Here

*Welcome. Take a breath. This is going to be okay.*

## Who this is for

You wrote a book. Or you sell stuff on eBay. Or you built a small business out
of your laptop and a lot of stubbornness. Money started showing up in accounts
that aren't a payroll department — and then you heard the words **Schedule C**
and felt cold.

This manual is for you. Specifically, if:

- You're a self-published author (KDP, IngramSpark, direct sales, audiobooks)
- You freelance or do gig work that issues you a 1099, not a W-2
- You run a single-member LLC whose activity shows up on your personal return
- You flip/resell (eBay, Amazon, garage sales that got ambitious)
- You tried reading IRS.gov and it felt like reading a firewall in a foreign language

## The one-paragraph version of your whole situation

You are a business now. The IRS doesn't primarily tax your *person* — it taxes
your **business's profit**: money in, minus ordinary and necessary expenses,
reported on a single form called Schedule C, which attaches to your normal
Form 1040. Your job all year is basically three things:

1. **Know what came in** (royalties, sales, freelance payments)
2. **Know what went out, and why** (expenses, with receipts)
3. **Set some aside as you go** (quarterly estimated payments) so tax season
   isn't a horror movie

That's it. Everything else in this manual is detail about those three things.

## Which sibling guide do you need?

Dylan's guides are a family, and they don't overlap much:

| You have... | Read... |
|---|---|
| A W-2 payroll job, confused about withholding | `quarterly-taxes-calm-guide` (the W-2 companion) |
| An LLC being *formed*, need the EIN/bank/registered agent walkthrough | `llc-first-year-handbook` |
| Schedule C income — royalties, freelance, reselling, side business | **This guide.** |

If you have a W-2 job *and* a side business, you'll touch both W-2 and
this guide — your normal job's withholding stays on the W-2 side, and your
book money lives here.

## How to use this manual

- **Read in order the first time.** Chapters were written to stack.
- Chapters are short on purpose. Skim headings first if your brain needs it.
- Every chapter ends with a **"Call a pro if..."** or points you to
  [06 — When to Call a Pro](06-when-to-call-a-pro.md). That part isn't
  boilerplate; it's the most important line on every page.
- Nothing in here is advice about *your* numbers. Taxes rhyme, but they
  don't repeat. Verify current rules at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov) and
  confirm with a tax professional before acting.

## The mindset shift that makes everything easier

Taxes aren't a test you fail. They're a **recipe with a lot of ingredients**.
The IRS doesn't care that the paperwork is boring — it only cares that it's
*accurate and honest*. A messy-but-honest filing beats a perfect system you
never actually keep. Start ugly, keep going, ask for help early.
