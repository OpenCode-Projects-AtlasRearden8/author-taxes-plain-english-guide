# 02 — Tracking Royalties

*A book's income arrives in weird little drips from five different companies.
Here's how to catch every drip.*

## The situation, honestly

An author's "income" is not one paycheck. It's a mosaic:

| Source | What it pays | Paperwork you'll see |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon KDP | eBooks, paperbacks, KU reads, ads | 1099 from Amazon; monthly statements |
| IngramSpark | Bookstores & libraries, wholesale | 1099-ish / annual statements |
| Findaway / ACX etc. | Audiobook sales | 1099s from the platform |
| Direct sales (Stripe, PayPal, gumroad-style) | Signed copies, bundles, PDFs, courses | Platform statements; 1099-K possibly |
| Affiliate / ad revenue | "Buy my book via this link" crumbs | 1099s or just statements |
| Freelance / speaking / workshops | Anything-author-adjacent | 1099-NEC from clients |

Each source is *separate* royalty income — all of it rolls up into one
**Gross receipts** number on Schedule C. The trap isn't forgetting the big
platform; it's forgetting the $34 dribble.

## Rule #1: track the *statement*, not the bank deposit

Deposits get lumped, netted, delayed, and split. Platforms' monthly
**statements** show the true picture: gross sales, platform fees, your net.
Record gross, fees, and net separately — because the fees are *deductible
expenses*, and deposits alone will hide them from you.

Practical habit (pick one, keep it):

- **Spreadsheet method (most beginners):** one sheet, one row per platform per
  month: `date | platform | gross | fees | net | deposit date | notes`.
- **Bookkeeping app method:** connect accounts, categorize deposits, and let
  the app do math. Fine, but still reconcile against the platform statements
  once a quarter.

## Rule #2: one bucket per money-personality

Whether you use apps or an envelope system:

1. **Business checking account** — *all* business money in and out. No
   personal spending from it, ever, even for $2.
2. **Tax-estimate account** — each time money lands, move a slice (Chapter 04
   helps you pick a habit, not a hard rule) here before you feel rich.
3. **Personal account** — after the slice is moved, you may transfer yourself
   an "owner's draw." That's not a wage or a taxable event *by itself*; your
   **profit** is taxable regardless of draws.

## Royalty-specific things that trip beginners

- **KDP "expanded distribution," foreign sales, and currency conversions:**
  platforms may pay in fragments and different currencies. Your spreadsheet
  row should reference the *statement period*, even if the cash lands a month
  later. (Accrual vs. cash is a pro conversation — most small authors use
  cash-basis: count it when it actually reaches you. A tax pro can confirm
  that fits your situation.)
- **Free months / Kindle Unlimited page reads:** they generate "payments" on
  statements that are royalties too. Log them; don't ignore rows that say $9.00.
- **Chargebacks/refunds on direct sales:** track so returns-and-allowances
  stays accurate.
- **1099 threshold games:** platforms may not send a 1099 if you earned below
  the current threshold. *You still report it.* Fear of the missing-1099
  year is why this chapter exists.
- **1099 vs. your math:** in January, pull every statement, list every 1099,
  and reconcile against your spreadsheet. Mismatches: call or message the
  platform — but still file accurately, noting discrepancies if the platform
  won't correct.

## A calm month-end ritual (10 minutes, tops)

Every last day of the month:

1. Sign into each platform. Note the month's gross, fees, net. (One row each.)
2. Match the net to actual deposits.
3. Move your estimate slice into the tax account.
4. File receipts you collected this month (Chapter 05 has the system).

That's it. You're not "doing taxes" — you're *notarizing the month* so future
-you doesn't have to archaeologize.

## Episode of "But what about...?"

- *"I reinvested all my royalties into ads — do I still owe tax?"* — Yes,
  profit is what's taxed; those ads are *expenses* that lower profit, which is
  the whole point of Chapter 03.
- *"I gave books away / got author copies free."* — Free promotional copies
  generally aren't income, but "author copy at a discount" rules exist and are
  subtle. Keep records; ask a pro before assuming either way.
- *"I sold through my LLC but banked in my personal checking."* — Fixable, but
  it's a commingling habit to quit *now*, not in April. See the LLC handbook.

## Call a pro when...

- You have income in multiple states or countries (foreign platforms,
  foreign withholding — real rabbit holes).
- You're deciding accrual vs. cash-basis accounting for real.
- Your annual numbers arrive and 1099s don't reconcile at all.
